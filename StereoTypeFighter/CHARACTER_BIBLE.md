# Stereotype Fighter — Character Bible

This document defines the 16-character roster shown in the two supplied **Select Your Fighter** reference images. The tone is deliberately exaggerated, cheesy and arcade-like: every fighter is a fictional stereotype, not a depiction of a real person.

For future art generation, each character should have two locked visual references:

1. **Retro reference** — preserve the costume, hair, body shape, accessories, country flag and stage cues from the supplied pixel-art roster card.
2. **Photoreal reference** — reinterpret the same fictional character as a believable real person without copying any celebrity or real individual.

Future sprite sheets should preserve those locked references across idle, walk, crouch, jump, punch, kick, block, hurt, knockdown, victory, taunt, special and super animations.

### RAPTHUG
**Archetype:** Gangster Rapper  
**Pronouns:** He/Him  
**Country:** USA  
**Home Stage:** Back Alley Cypher

**Backstory:** Rapthug was a battle rapper with one regional hit, six unreleased mixtapes and a permanent belief that the industry is afraid of him. When a rival released a diss track called *Soft Bars*, he entered Stereotype Fighter to settle the argument somewhere comments could not be deleted. He wants the championship belt because he is convinced it will look perfect on his next album cover.

**Fighting Style:** Heavy street-boxing, shoulder feints and rhythm-timed punches.

**Special — Bass Drop:** Rapthug stomps on the beat and sends a ground-hugging bass shockwave across the stage.

**Super — Platinum Combo:** A rapid punch sequence synced to an imaginary beat, ending in a huge microphone uppercut and a freeze-frame album-cover pose.

**Personality:** Cool until challenged, then completely incapable of letting anything go.

**Photoreal art direction:** Original fictional Black American rapper in his 30s, athletic heavyweight build, black cap and hoodie, dark loose jeans, heavy gold chain, tan work boots, gritty brick alley, dramatic realistic street photography, same silhouette and attitude as the pixel-art reference, no celebrity resemblance.

### KILLWOODRAT
**Archetype:** Toxic Alt Girl  
**Pronouns:** She/Her  
**Country:** Canada  
**Home Stage:** The Back-Lane Venue

**Backstory:** Killwoodrat has been banned from three music forums, two rehearsal spaces and one very specific record store. She collects obscure horror tapes, patched black clothes and grudges with impressive dedication. She joined the tournament after somebody described her playlist as “pretty mainstream actually,” and has been furious ever since.

**Fighting Style:** Fast close-range rushdown, low kicks, elbows and theatrical slash-like arcade effects.

**Special — Razor Riff:** A spinning rush attack that leaves a jagged metallic soundwave in its wake.

**Super — Blacklist Breakdown:** The lights flicker, the stage drops into concert-darkness, and she unloads a frantic chain of kicks and strikes before the lights snap back on.

**Personality:** Sarcastic, intense, musically judgmental and secretly delighted to be here.

**Photoreal art direction:** Original fictional Canadian alt woman in her 20s, short black choppy hair, black crop top and distressed shorts, fishnets, combat boots, dark eyeliner, grungy alley venue, realistic photography, preserve the pixel-art clothing and silhouette, no celebrity resemblance.

### YUPPIE
**Archetype:** Finance Bro

**Home Stage:** Harbour Capital

**Photoreal art direction:** Original fictional American finance professional in his 30s, tailored navy suit, white shirt, red tie, polished shoes, leather briefcase, waterfront financial-district skyline, athletic but office-polished look, cinematic realistic photography, no celebrity resemblance.

### FAT GAMER
**Archetype:** Pro Gamer  
**Pronouns:** He/Him  
**Country:** USA  
**Home Stage:** The Gaming Den

**Backstory:** FAT GAMER has thousands of tournament hours, frightening matchup knowledge and exactly zero interest in cardio that does not involve a loading screen. After his chat spent years claiming he could never survive a real fighting tournament, he accepted the challenge purely to prove that frame data beats fitness. To everyone’s surprise, he is extremely good.

**Fighting Style:** Defensive reads, explosive counters and deceptively powerful close-range attacks.

**Special — Level Up:** A burst of pixel effects temporarily boosts his speed and damage after he correctly counters an attack.

**Super — Raid Boss:** The room flashes GAME OVER as he absorbs a hit, powers through it and delivers a huge multi-hit finishing combo.

**Personality:** Competitive, knowledgeable, funny under pressure and always blaming lag.

**Photoreal art direction:** Original fictional heavyset American male pro gamer in his 20s or 30s, black glasses, headset, black LEVEL UP T-shirt, grey sweatpants, sneakers, cluttered gaming room with retro PC and snacks, realistic documentary photography, confident rather than demeaning portrayal, no celebrity resemblance.

### JUNKIE
**Archetype:** Tweaker  
**Pronouns:** He/Him  
**Country:** USA  
**Home Stage:** The Forgotten Block

**Backstory:** Nobody knows where JUNKIE came from, although several people insist they have seen him fixing a shopping trolley at 4 a.m. He is a fictional arcade caricature of chaotic street energy rather than a realistic portrayal of addiction. He wandered into the qualifiers after hearing there was prize money, free food and somewhere warm to sit — then accidentally beat three seeded fighters.

**Fighting Style:** Unpredictable movement, awkward angles, sudden bursts and evasive scrambles.

**Special — Street Static:** A jittery fake-out dash that can switch direction at the last moment.

**Super — No Sleep Till KO:** A wild flurry of erratic movement and fast strikes that is almost impossible to read but leaves him exhausted afterward.

**Personality:** Restless, strangely resourceful and impossible to predict.

**Photoreal art direction:** Original fictional weathered American street character, black knit beanie, layered dark jacket and hoodie, distressed jeans, worn boots, scruffy beard, grimy urban service alley and shopping cart in background, grounded realistic photography without glamorising drug use, no celebrity resemblance.

## Art Consistency Rules

- Treat every roster portrait as an **original fictional person**; do not imitate celebrities or recognisable real people.
- Preserve each fighter's main hair shape, costume palette, major accessories, approximate body type and stage theme from the supplied pixel art.
- The photoreal version becomes the high-detail identity reference; the arcade sprite becomes the gameplay silhouette reference.
- Keep facial features consistent across all future portraits and sprite generations.
- Avoid radically changing age, body shape, ethnicity presentation, hairstyle or costume between animations.
- Sprite sheets should use consistent frame dimensions, transparent backgrounds, fixed foot baseline and predictable frame ordering.
- Each fighter should eventually receive: idle, walk forward/back, crouch, jump, block, light/heavy punch, light/heavy kick, throw, hurt, knockdown, get-up, taunt, victory, special, super and KO animations.
